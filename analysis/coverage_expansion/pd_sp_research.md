## PART 2: Specific Plan (SP) Zones

### 2.1 What is a Specific Plan Zone?

**Definition:** A Specific Plan zone provides for the classification and development of land as a coordinated, comprehensive project with customized development regulations that supersede conventional zoning.

**Purpose:**
- Allow flexibility for innovation in building types
- Enable mixed-use development
- Coordinate large-scale comprehensive projects
- Provide site-specific development standards

**Key Characteristic:** Each SP zone has **unique regulations** defined in its specific plan document.

## PART 3: Analysis & Recommendations

### 3.1 Existing FAR Summary

**PD Zones:**
- PD-30: 0.30 FAR (observed)
- PD: 0.20 FAR (observed)
- PCD-1: 0.27 FAR (observed)
- **Range:** 0.20 - 0.30 FAR

**SP Zones:**
- Typical range: 0.20 - 0.35 FAR
- High-density residential: up to 0.60 FAR (SP-2-SFR)
- Commercial: up to 0.38 FAR (SP-City Ranch)
- **Range:** 0.20 - 0.60 FAR

### 3.2 Challenges with PD/SP Zones

**1. Conditional Use Permit Dependency**
- PD zones require approved CUP for most development
- CUP establishes FAR, density, height on case-by-case basis
- No single "standard" FAR exists

**2. Specific Plan Variability**
- Each SP has unique regulations
- Standards vary by jurisdiction and plan purpose
- 60+ different SP zone codes in database
- Would require 60+ separate specific plan document reviews

**3. Data Limitations**
- Database only shows "SP" or "SP-[Name]" without plan details
- Actual allowable FAR not stored in property records
- Existing FAR may not reflect maximum allowable FAR

**4. Regulatory Complexity**
- PD: Governed by CUP + Title 22 base standards
- SP: Governed by individual specific plan document
- Both override conventional zoning rules

## PART 4: Key Findings Summary

### Regulatory Framework
1. **PD zones** use Conditional Use Permits to establish development standards on a case-by-case basis
2. **SP zones** are governed by individual specific plan documents unique to each jurisdiction
3. **Base standards exist** (35 ft height, 5,000 sqft min lot) but are frequently modified
4. **FAR is rarely specified** in base regulations - established by CUP or specific plan

### Development Patterns
1. **Existing development is underutilized:** Most PD/SP zones show 0.20-0.35 FAR
2. **Large lot sizes:** Average 20,000-90,000 sqft for PD zones
3. **Small buildings:** Average 1,600-3,500 sqft building size
4. **Potential for intensification:** Existing FAR well below typical zoning maximums

### Coverage Opportunity
1. **76,000+ properties** could be covered by adding PD/SP zones
2. **5-6% of total database** - significant coverage improvement
3. **Low implementation complexity** for base PD/SP zones
4. **High research cost** for jurisdiction-specific SP standards
